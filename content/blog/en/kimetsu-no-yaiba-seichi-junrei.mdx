## "Demon Slayer" and the Pilgrimage Phenomenon

"Demon Slayer: Kimetsu no Yaiba," based on the manga by Gotouge Koyoharu, became a full-blown cultural phenomenon after the anime began airing in 2019. The theatrical release "Mugen Train" went on to break Japan's all-time box-office record. Along with the series' explosive popularity, shrines and landmarks across the country that share connections to the characters and story have drawn attention from fans as pilgrimage destinations.

The series is set in the Taisho era and is a work of fiction -- no official real-world locations have been announced. However, places with strong ties to character names and visual motifs from the show can be found nationwide, and many fans visit them as part of their own pilgrimages.

## Fukuoka: Homangu Kamado Shrine

### The Shrine That Shares Its Name with Tanjiro

Homangu Kamado Shrine in Dazaifu, Fukuoka Prefecture, bears the same "Kamado" name as protagonist Kamado Tanjiro, making it the single most famous Demon Slayer pilgrimage site. Already known as a matchmaking shrine, its visitor numbers surged following the anime's success.

- **Enshrined deity**: Tamayori-hime no Mikoto
- **Address**: 883 Uchiyama, Dazaifu, Fukuoka
- **Access**: About 10 minutes by community bus from Nishitetsu Dazaifu Station, or roughly 40 minutes on foot
- **Tip**: Charms and talismans from the shrine are popular among Demon Slayer fans

### What to See

The shrine grounds sit at the foot of Mt. Homan, surrounded by rich greenery. In autumn, the foliage is stunning, and the observation deck offers a panoramic view of the Dazaifu cityscape. Visiting alongside Dazaifu Tenmangu is highly recommended. You will also find countless votive tablets (ema) with Demon Slayer artwork drawn by fans -- a testament to the series' devoted following.

## Nara: Katsuraki-ni-imasu-Honoikazuchi Shrine

### A Connection to Tanjiro's "Hinokami Kagura"

Katsuraki-ni-imasu-Honoikazuchi Shrine in Katsuragi City, Nara Prefecture, is an ancient shrine dedicated to the fire deity Honoikazuchi no Okami. Fans have drawn connections between the shrine and Tanjiro's "Hinokami Kagura" technique, as well as the Kamado family's lineage as charcoal makers.

- **Address**: 448 Fuefuki, Katsuragi City, Nara
- **Access**: About 25 minutes on foot from Oshimi Station on the Kintetsu Gose Line
- **Note**: Located in a mountainous area, so bring warm clothing in winter

Considered one of Japan's oldest shrines, it exudes a quiet solemnity. The atmosphere evokes the Taisho-era Japan that forms the backdrop of the series.

## Tokyo and Saitama: Mt. Kumotori

### The Model for the Kamado Family's Home

Mt. Kumotori (elevation 2,017 m), straddling the borders of Tokyo, Saitama, and Yamanashi, is believed to be the model for Tanjiro's mountain hometown. The opening scene where Tanjiro descends the mountain to sell charcoal has inspired many fans to combine a mountain hike with their pilgrimage.

- **Elevation**: 2,017 m (the highest peak in Tokyo)
- **Main route**: Kamosawa route (roughly 4 to 5 hours one way)
- **Access**: Take a bus from JR Ome Line Okutama Station to the Kamosawa bus stop
- **Difficulty**: Intermediate. A day hike is possible, but an overnight stay at a mountain hut is recommended

### Important Notes for Hikers

Mt. Kumotori requires a proper mountain hike. Even if you are visiting as a pilgrimage, full hiking gear is essential.

- Bring hiking boots, rain gear, and warm layers
- Carry sufficient water and food
- Submit a climbing plan before you go
- If the weather turns bad, do not hesitate to turn back

## Tokyo: Mt. Otake

Mt. Otake (elevation 1,266 m) sits in the Okutama area and is notable for its distinctive summit shape. It is said to be one of the models for the mountains depicted in the series. With lower difficulty than Mt. Kumotori, it is a more approachable option for hiking beginners who want to incorporate a pilgrimage.

- **Access**: Take a bus and cable car from JR Ome Line Mitake Station
- **Hiking time**: About 2 hours one way via Mt. Mitake
- **Tip**: Combining the hike with a visit to Musashi Mitake Shrine on Mt. Mitake makes for a full and rewarding day

## Tochigi: Ashikaga Flower Park

### A Spectacular Wisteria Display

In "Demon Slayer," wisteria flowers play an important role as blossoms that demons despise. Ashikaga Flower Park in Ashikaga City, Tochigi Prefecture, boasts some of Japan's largest wisteria trellises and has gained pilgrimage-like popularity among fans who liken it to the Wisteria Mountain from the Final Selection arc.

- **Address**: 607 Hasamacho, Ashikaga City, Tochigi
- **Access**: 3-minute walk from Ashikaga Flower Park Station on the JR Ryomo Line
- **Wisteria season**: Mid-April to mid-May
- **Admission**: Varies by season (approximately 2,200 yen during wisteria season)

During peak bloom, wisteria in shades of purple, white, and pink cascade throughout the park, creating a truly otherworldly scene that feels like stepping into the anime. Evening illumination events add a completely different layer of mystical beauty.

## Suggested Pilgrimage Routes

Since Demon Slayer locations are scattered across the country, it makes sense to tackle them by region.

### Kanto Area (1 Night / 2 Days)

1. **Day 1**: Hike Mt. Otake in the Okutama area, explore around Mt. Mitake
2. **Day 2**: Enjoy Ashikaga Flower Park (wisteria season only)

### Kyushu and Kansai Area (1 Night / 2 Days)

1. **Day 1**: Visit Homangu Kamado Shrine and Dazaifu Tenmangu in Fukuoka
2. **Day 2**: Visit Katsuraki-ni-imasu-Honoikazuchi Shrine in Nara, stroll through Nara Park
