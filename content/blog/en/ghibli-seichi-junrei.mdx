## Studio Ghibli and Anime Pilgrimages

Studio Ghibli films have captivated audiences worldwide with their loving depictions of Japan's natural beauty and cultural heritage. Director Hayao Miyazaki and the creative team frequently draw on real locations for their settings, resulting in Ghibli "pilgrimage sites" scattered across the country.

In this article, we introduce the must-visit Ghibli pilgrimage destinations along with practical access information.

## Ghibli Museum, Mitaka -- The Starting Point for Any Ghibli Pilgrimage

### Essential Information

No discussion of Ghibli pilgrimages is complete without the Ghibli Museum in Mitaka, Tokyo. Directed by Hayao Miyazaki himself, this one-of-a-kind facility lets you step into the world of Ghibli films.

- **Address**: 1-1-83 Shimorenjaku, Mitaka, Tokyo
- **Access**: About 5 minutes by bus from the south exit of Mitaka Station (JR Chuo Line), or roughly 15 minutes on foot
- **Admission**: 1,000 yen for adults (timed-entry reservation required)
- **Note**: Reservations are mandatory -- tickets must be purchased in advance through Lawson Ticket

### Highlights

- The rooftop Robot Soldier is an essential photo spot
- Short films shown here are exclusive to the museum
- The Catbus play area is a hit with children (adults can't ride, unfortunately)
- Cafe "Straw Hat" offers dining immersed in the Ghibli aesthetic

## Tokorozawa Area -- The Setting of My Neighbor Totoro

### Sayama Hills and Totoro's Forest

The setting of "My Neighbor Totoro" is based on the Sayama Hills area near Tokorozawa, Saitama Prefecture. The satoyama woodlands are preserved as "Totoro's Forest," where you can experience the same countryside landscapes that Satsuki and Mei ran through.

- **Totoro's Forest (Sayama Hills)**: A satoyama woodland maintained by the Totoro no Furusato Foundation. Walking trails are well-maintained
- **Kurosuke's House**: A renovated farmhouse over 100 years old that evokes the world of Totoro (open on select days only)
- **Access**: Bus from Kotesashi Station on the Seibu Ikebukuro Line, or walk from Seibu-Kyujo-mae Station on the Seibu Sayama Line

### Matsugou Area

The Matsugou area of Tokorozawa is said to be the model for the neighborhood where Satsuki and Mei's house stands. Farmland still remains here, with peaceful rural scenery stretching in every direction.

- **Walking tip**: Stroll along the rice paddy paths and layer the real scenery over scenes from the film
- **Best season**: Early summer after the rice has been planted, or autumn when the rice stalks turn golden

## Dogo Onsen -- Spirited Away

### The Hot Spring Town That Inspired the Bathhouse

Dogo Onsen Honkan in Matsuyama, Ehime Prefecture, is widely cited as one of the inspirations for the bathhouse in "Spirited Away." This three-story wooden building, constructed in the Meiji era, has a grandeur that unmistakably recalls the world of the film.

- **Address**: 5-6 Dogo Yunomachi, Matsuyama, Ehime
- **Access**: 5-minute walk from Dogo Onsen Station on the Iyo Railway
- **Bathing fee**: From 460 yen (Kami-no-Yu, lower floor)
- **Highlight**: The nighttime illumination creates a fantastical atmosphere that makes the bathhouse connection feel even stronger

### Nearby Recommended Spots

- **Dogo Onsen Shopping Arcade (Dogo Haikara-dori)**: Lined with souvenir shops and street food vendors
- **Dogo Onsen Annex Asuka-no-Yu**: A newer bathing facility that blends art with hot springs
- **Matsuyama Castle**: A landmark worth visiting alongside Dogo Onsen

## Yakushima -- The Forest of Princess Mononoke

### The Moss-Covered Forest of Shiratani Unsuikyo

The forests of Yakushima, Kagoshima Prefecture, are widely known as the model for the forests in "Princess Mononoke." Shiratani Unsuikyo Ravine features an otherworldly primeval forest blanketed in moss. It's a place where you can feel the mystical atmosphere of the Forest Spirit's domain in the film.

- **Access**: Bus from Yakushima Airport to Miyanoura Port, then bus to Shiratani Unsuikyo trailhead (about 30 minutes)
- **Duration**: About 1 hour one way to the Moss Forest (roughly 3 hours round trip)
- **Entry fee**: 500 yen forest environment maintenance fee
- **Gear**: Trekking shoes are essential. Rain gear is a must (Yakushima gets heavy rainfall)

### Ancient Cedars and Primeval Forest

If you continue past Shiratani Unsuikyo to Taikobashi Rock, you'll be rewarded with a sweeping panoramic view of Yakushima's mountains. For those with the stamina, it's well worth the effort.

## Other Ghibli Pilgrimage Sites

### Ghibli Spots Scattered Across Japan

- **Tomonoura, Hiroshima (Ponyo on the Cliff by the Sea)**: The port town where Miyazaki stayed to develop the film's concept. Its hillside streets and ocean views are memorable
- **Jimbocho, Tokyo (Whisper of the Heart)**: The antiquarian bookshop district shares a kinship with the film's atmosphere
- **Azumino, Nagano (Only Yesterday)**: Beautiful countryside with the Northern Alps as a backdrop
- **Seiseki-Sakuragaoka, Tokyo (Whisper of the Heart)**: The area around Seiseki-Sakuragaoka Station on the Keio Line served as the model for the film's setting
